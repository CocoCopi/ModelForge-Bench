"Did you find the place all right?" she asked, holding the door open with her
shoulder. "The street signs are terrible around here."

"I found it eventually," he said. "I walked past it twice. The number is
painted on the wrong side of the wall."

"That is my landlord's contribution to urban design." She stepped aside.
"There is coffee in the kitchen, and possibly a chair that does not wobble."

The flat was small and warm, with books stacked in the hallway because the
shelves had run out of room years ago. A cat looked at him from the arm of the
sofa, decided he was not interesting, and went back to sleep.

"So," she said, sitting down opposite him. "You said on the phone that you
wanted to ask me something, and then you did not ask it."

He turned his cup around on the table. "I wanted to know why you left the
project. The official reason was very official."

She laughed without much humour. "The official reason was that I was needed
elsewhere. The real reason is that I was tired of being the person who says no
to every clever idea at the last possible moment."

"And now?"

"Now I am the person who says no from a different building." She looked out
of the window at the rain. "It is quieter here. I sleep better. I am not sure
that is progress, but it is something."

He nodded slowly. Outside, a bus went past with its lights on, and the room
grew a little darker. "I think it is progress," he said. "I think saying no
from a place where you can sleep is probably the whole trick."
