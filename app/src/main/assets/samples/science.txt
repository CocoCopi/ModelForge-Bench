Water is unusual because its solid form floats. Most substances become denser
as they freeze, but ice is less dense than liquid water, so it forms on the
surface of a pond and insulates the water below. That layer is why fish can
survive a cold winter while the air above the ice falls far below freezing.

Light travels more slowly through glass than through a vacuum. When a beam
enters at an angle, one side of the wavefront slows before the other, and the
beam bends. This is refraction, and it is the reason a straw looks broken in a
glass of water and why a lens can focus sunlight onto a single bright point.

The atmosphere presses on everything at about one kilogram per square
centimetre at sea level. We do not notice it because the pressure inside the
body pushes outward by the same amount. A sudden change in altitude, however,
makes the difference obvious: ears pop as the pressure on each side of the
eardrum takes a moment to equalise.

Electric current is the movement of charge, not the movement of energy. In a
simple circuit, electrons drift slowly while the electromagnetic field carries
energy around the loop at a large fraction of the speed of light. This is why
a lamp lights almost instantly even though no single electron has travelled
far in that time.

Sound needs a medium. A bell ringing inside a jar grows quieter as the air is
pumped out, until only the vibration through the table remains. Space is not
silent because nothing is there to make noise, but because there is nothing
there to carry it. Explosions in films are an artistic choice, not physics.
